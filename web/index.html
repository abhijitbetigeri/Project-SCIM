The loop
01

Decided

A multi-agent mesh negotiates the shortage: transfer near-expiry surplus between branches first, then buy only the net shortfall. One approval. This half already works.

02

Done

The unsolved half. A robot executes that ticket in real space — navigating a cramped service pass, selecting the right crate, handing it to a cook who isn't looking at it.

03

Learned

Every run emits trajectories, zone transitions, pick and place events, and handoff timing. The dataset a back-of-house restock policy trains on.

Why this is a robotics project

The behaviours nobody has data for

Manipulation datasets are mostly tabletop. Warehouse datasets assume wide aisles and no humans underfoot. A restaurant back-of-house is sub-metre clearances, a moving crew, and objects whose identity matters — the near-expiry crate is not interchangeable with the fresh one.

zoneIdentifierAccesses

Entry and exit at each branch and the warehouse dock — task phase boundaries.

interactionEventRecords

pick_begin → pick_complete → place_begin → place_complete. Manipulation segmentation.

gamePlayEventRecords

Ticket assigned, task complete, scenario resolved. Success labelling.

handoffEvents

Approach distance and release delay at the cook. The collaboration timing distribution — the specific gap this addresses.

trajectory + action label

Pose, speed and a discrete action per sample, exported as CSV.

consent by construction

Simulated only. No real kitchens, no identifiable people — a working kitchen is a private space whose staff cannot consent frame by frame.
